# Ideal Customer Profile: Hubtel Business Payments and Back-Office Offer

**Owner:** Go-to-Market Engineering | **Audience:** Commercial and leadership teams | **Version:** 2026-W46
Related: [Buying committee](buying-committee.md) | [Qualification framework](icp-qualification-framework.md) | [Research and source register](../docs/research/source-register.md) (S# = source ID)

## Summary

**Primary target:** owner-led hospitality and food-service businesses in Greater Accra with 2 to 15 outlets. These are hotels and serviced apartments, restaurant and cafe groups, spas and salons, and venues that take customer-present payments through cash, mobile money and card.

**Secondary target:** owner-led multi-branch retail (pharmacies, supermarkets, fashion, hardware), once the evidence gaps below are closed.

**Why hospitality first:**
- Our best small-business proof points sit here: a hotel in East Legon and a spa and salon, both quoted in Hubtel case studies (S50, S51).
- Their pains match what our Clarity, Control and Growth pages already say: unconfirmed payments, branch figures that do not agree, slow settlement, and staff handling money (S2, S3).
- Signals for choosing accounts are publicly visible: new outlets, venue launches, reviews and job posts.

**Focus:** this ICP covers one motion, which is selling the business payments and back-office offer to owner-led, multi-outlet businesses. It does not cover enterprise collections, the marketplace or consumers.

## Evidence and assumptions

Established evidence is what Hubtel's own pages and published case studies say, plus third-party sources in the register. Strategic assumptions are our working choices, to be tested in the first campaign. They are marked **(A)** below.

Three limits to keep in mind:
- Only two small-business customers are named, and both stories are Hubtel-written.
- Outlet and employee ranges are our design choices, not findings.
- Branch dashboards, per-staff sales tracking, automated reconciliation and audit logs are advertised on the homepage but not documented in the material reviewed. **Confirm with Product before any message promises them.**

## Primary ICP: multi-outlet hospitality and food service

### Business profile

| Attribute | Profile |
|---|---|
| Industry | Hotels and serviced apartments, restaurant and cafe groups, spas and salons, event venues, catering and bakeries with outlets |
| Ownership | Privately owned and owner-led, with the owner or a general manager close to daily decisions |
| Size | 2 to 15 outlets **(A)**. About 15 to 250 employees **(A)**. No revenue threshold is set because no evidence supports one |
| Geography | Greater Accra first (Accra, Tema). Other cities later, where Hubtel's presence is confirmed. Hubtel states it operates in 8+ cities (S1) |
| Operations | Several shifts. Cash, mobile money and card at the counter. Staff handle money. The owner is not at every outlet or shift. Takings are consolidated by hand |
| Technology | Low to medium maturity. Already accepts mobile money and card or POS. Not yet on integrated back-office tools **(A)** |
| Not a fit | Businesses that only need to collect mobile money. Hubtel's homepage says the offer is for "scale-ready businesses" (S1) |

### Needs and pain points

| Pain | Basis |
|---|---|
| Cannot confirm each payment arrived (checking MoMo, bank apps, screenshots) | Hubtel's stated problem (S2) |
| Branch figures arrive late and in different formats. Takings do not match the bank | Hubtel's stated problem (S2) |
| Slow settlement. The hotel waited one to three weeks with a previous provider | Hotel case study (S51) |
| Payment failures at the till and loss of staff time fixing them | Spa and salon case study (S50) |
| Staff handle money without a clear approval trail | Hubtel's stated problem (S3) |
| Payroll and supplier payments done manually | Hubtel built payroll for BackOffice (S47). Pain in this segment is **(A)** |

The outcomes buyers want are to see takings per outlet daily, get money faster, have fewer failed or disputed payments, and control who approves money leaving the business. These are **(A)** until confirmed in customer conversations.

### Relevant Hubtel solutions

Documented in Hubtel's own material:

| Need | Solution |
|---|---|
| Faster access to money | Hourly balance crediting and sweeps (S48) |
| Control over outgoing money | Money page with owner and finance-manager approval roles (S46). Bulk payroll with an approver (S47) |
| Taking payments at the counter | POS for cash, card and mobile money (S51) |
| Payments when the internet is down | USSD without internet (S50) |
| Integration with existing systems | APIs (S5) |

Not yet confirmed: branch-level reporting, per-staff sales tracking, automated reconciliation and audit logs. They are the core of the "see every outlet" promise, so confirming them with Product is the first dependency for this ICP.

### Decision-makers

Detail is in the [buying committee](buying-committee.md). In short:
- **Economic buyer:** the owner or managing director. The hotel case quotes a CEO (S51).
- **Champion and evaluator:** the general manager, operations lead or finance and accounts lead. Hubtel's Money page already has a finance-manager role (S46).
- **Technical check:** often no IT function. It falls to the finance lead, a POS contact or the owner **(A)**.
- **Adoption risk:** cashiers, front-desk staff and outlet managers. The spa case quotes a front-desk manager (S50).

### Buying triggers

All are **(A)** unless noted. Each is something we can watch for.

| Trigger | Where to see it |
|---|---|
| Opening a second or later outlet | Social posts, press, Google listings |
| Payment failures or slow service at the till | Reviews, social posts |
| Frustration with a current provider's settlement time (confirmed in the hotel case, S51) | Conversations |
| Hiring for finance, accounts or cashier roles | Job posts |
| New venue, franchise or expansion announcement | News, social |
| BackOffice take-payment features once launched (Hubtel has said "will soon be ready", S49) | Hubtel product team |

### Qualification criteria

An account qualifies when it meets **all** the following:
1. Privately owned and owner-led, with 2 to 15 outlets in the target geography.
2. Already accepts mobile money and either card or POS, so it is past the "just collecting mobile money" stage.
3. Shows at least one pain from the table above, from conversation or public evidence.
4. Its main pain is something Hubtel can solve today, per the documented solutions.
5. Has a reachable decision-maker (owner, general manager or finance lead).
6. Is a verified, operating business.

Exclude the account if any of these apply:
- It is an existing Hubtel customer. New-customer acquisition does not target them.
- It is a single outlet with no expansion plan.
- It is part of a large group with central procurement.
- Its only relationship with Hubtel is as a Hubtel Food or marketplace partner.

The scoring and CRM fields are in the [qualification framework](icp-qualification-framework.md).

## Secondary ICP: multi-branch retail

Owner-led retailers with 2 to 15 shops (pharmacies, supermarkets and minimarts, fashion, hardware, electronics) have the pain Hubtel's homepage describes most directly: branch reporting mismatches, cash leakage and supplier payment control (S2, S3). We have **not** yet found a named retail customer or a documented branch or reconciliation capability. The Retailer BackOffice is built for marketplace sellers, not for retail operations (S45).

Retail moves into the primary ICP once Product confirms the branch and reconciliation capabilities and we have at least one retail reference. Until then, we run it as a smaller test alongside hospitality. Buyer roles and qualification follow the same model.

## Out of scope for the initial campaign

| Segment | Status | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Marketplace sellers (Sell on Hubtel, Hubtel Food partners) | Excluded | A different motion, where Hubtel recruits supply for its own marketplace |
| Gaming businesses | Excluded | Out of scope for this campaign. Betting is a subset and stays out with it |
| Large enterprise accounts (large collectors, disbursers, agent networks) | Excluded | Best-evidenced revenue area (S33 to S35), but delivered as custom projects with long procurement cycles, not a repeatable outbound motion |
| Utilities, betting and regulated financial institutions | Deferred | Revisit after the first campaign. Utilities are a sensitive sector given public coverage of the ECG arrangement (S42, S43) |
| Developer-led e-commerce, micro and informal merchants, single-outlet businesses | Not targeted | Crowded market or too small for the offer (S1, S16, S24) |
| Existing Hubtel customers | Excluded from acquisition | Handled through account management |

## Confidence

- Segment choice and exclusions: medium.
- Outlet and employee ranges: low (design choices).
- Pains, triggers and buyer roles: low to medium (Hubtel's own framing and two case studies, with no buyer interviews yet).

## Implications for the campaign

1. Choose accounts on structure (outlets, payment channels, staff handling money) and on evidence of pain, not on industry label alone.
2. Make the first campaign a test of the assumptions as well as a sales effort. Record which pains prospects confirm.
3. Position only on capabilities Product has confirmed.
4. Keep claims to what Hubtel can evidence, and attribute figures to Hubtel.
