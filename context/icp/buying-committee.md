# Buying Committee: Owner-Led Multi-Outlet Businesses

**Owner:** Go-to-Market Engineering | **Audience:** GTM, Sales and leadership | **Version:** 2026-W46
Applies to the primary ICP (hospitality and food service) and, with adjustments, the secondary ICP (retail) in [ideal-customer-profile.md](ideal-customer-profile.md). S# = source ID in the [source register](../docs/research/source-register.md).

Roles, priorities and objections below are our working model. We will refine them through customer conversations and early campaign results.

## Summary

In owner-led, multi-outlet Ghanaian businesses the buying committee is small, informal and unevenly documented. The **economic buyer is most likely the owner or managing director**, who also often acts as the functional buyer. A **finance or accounts lead and outlet managers** are the real evaluators of whether a payments and back-office tool works. **Cashiers and front-desk staff use it every day and can block adoption.** There is probably **no dedicated IT function**, so the technical evaluator is often the finance lead, an outside POS or software contact, or nobody.

**Evidence base.** Almost everything here is hypothesis. What is verified is narrow:
- Hubtel's Control page addresses "business owners" and describes owner approval (S3, V that it says so).
- Hubtel's BackOffice names an owner with approval rights and a "finance manager" role who tracks pending payment requests (S46, V).
- Hubtel's payroll feature lets the account owner give approval rights to others (S47, V).
- Named quotes in case studies come from a hotel **CEO** (S51) and a spa **front desk manager** (S50). Both are vendor-written.
- Digital-payment adoption barriers cited in a secondary source: fraud concerns, uncertainty about returns, taxation (S24, snippet-level).

We have no buyer-side interviews, job-title data or procurement studies yet. Titles and priorities below are **H** (our assumptions) unless marked. Hubtel's Sales and Customer Success teams can confirm or correct them from real deals.

## Evidence labels

**V** verified that a source says it (vendor-sourced unless stated). **I** inference, reasoning shown. **H** hypothesis to test.

## Committee overview

| Role | Likely title(s) | Influence on purchase | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Economic buyer | Owner, managing director, CEO | Decides | **V**: CEO quoted in the hotel case (S51). **H** that this holds across the segment |
| Functional buyer | General manager, operations director, finance or accounts manager | Shapes and champions | **V** for the "finance manager" role in Hubtel's product (S46). **H** otherwise |
| Technical evaluator | Often the finance lead, an IT contractor or POS vendor, or the owner personally | Can veto on feasibility | **H** |
| End users | Cashiers, front-desk staff, waiters, shop attendants, outlet managers | Make or break adoption | **V**: front desk manager quoted (S50) |
| Internal influencers | Outlet managers, head cashier, co-owner or family member, external accountant | Advise the owner | **H** |
| Potential blockers | Incumbent provider, cashiers, outlet managers, bank or card terminal contract, tax-averse owner | Delay or stop | **H**, with partial support from S24 |

Committee size (H): 2 to 5 people. Many deals may be decided by one person.

## 1. Economic buyer: owner or managing director

| Aspect | Description | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Responsibilities** | Sets direction, approves spending, holds the cash and the risk. Often involved in daily operations in an owner-led business | **I** |
| **Business priorities** | Protect cash flow. Grow to more outlets without losing control. Keep customers satisfied. Know if money is leaking | **V** that Hubtel frames these (S2, S3). **H** that owners rank them this way |
| **Pain points** | Cannot see the money without asking staff. Dependence on key people. Slow settlement. Payment verification effort | **V** that Hubtel names them (S2, S3). **V** for slow settlement (hotel CEO, S51, vendor-written) |
| **Buying motivations** | Control, trust in the numbers, speed of access to money, fewer disputes | **H** |
| **Likely objections** | Cost and fees (no public pricing exists, S52, S53). "My current provider works." Switching disruption. Trust in a platform holding settlement funds. Fear of tax visibility (barrier cited in S24, snippet-level). Fraud fears (S24) | **H**, partly **V** (S24) |
| **Influence** | Final decision. May delegate evaluation | **I** |
| **Messaging that would resonate (direction, not copy)** | Money you can see per outlet; faster access to money; fewer arguments about whether a payment arrived. Only claim capabilities that are documented (see capability warning) | **H** |
| **Messaging to avoid** | Large unsourced numbers; claims of branch dashboards or automated reconciliation until proven; any "customers like you" claim without a named, verified customer | Rule |

## 2. Functional buyer: general manager, operations lead or finance and accounts manager

| Aspect | Description | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Responsibilities** | Runs daily operations or the books. Collates outlet figures. Manages payroll and supplier payments. Handles disputes with customers and staff | **I**. **V** that Hubtel's Money page has a finance manager role (S46) |
| **Business priorities** | Accurate numbers on time. Fewer reconciliation errors. Clean payroll and supplier payments. Not being blamed when figures disagree | **H** |
| **Pain points** | Four outlets, four spreadsheets; chasing managers for reports; matching MoMo, bank and cash (S2, V that Hubtel says it) | **V** (Hubtel's words). **H** for this segment |
| **Buying motivations** | Time saved each week; fewer errors; an approval trail that protects them | **H** |
| **Likely objections** | Migration effort; double entry with existing tools; whether Hubtel's reports match their accounting format; learning curve; "will this make my job visible and risky?" | **H** |
| **Influence** | High. Often drafts the shortlist and recommends to the owner | **I** |
| **Messaging that would resonate** | Less time chasing reports; a clear record of who approved what (only if documented, payroll approvals and Money page requests are, S46 and S47); easier payroll. Avoid claiming reconciliation automation | **H** |

## 3. Technical evaluator: finance lead, outside IT or POS contact, or the owner

| Aspect | Description | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Responsibilities** | Checks that the tool works with existing till, internet, accounting tools and bank accounts | **H** |
| **Business priorities** | Reliability at the till; works without stable internet; low support burden | **V** for the spa: reliability and no-internet use were the stated pain (S50, vendor-written) |
| **Pain points** | Connectivity failures; terminals tied to a bank; integration with existing POS or accounting | **V** (spa, S50). **H** otherwise |
| **Buying motivations** | Fewer failures and support calls | **H** |
| **Likely objections** | "What happens when the network is down?" Integration with accounting or stock tools. Data export and ownership. Hubtel's uptime record (not independently verified) | **H** |
| **Influence** | Veto on feasibility. Rarely initiates | **H** |
| **Messaging that would resonate** | Works through USSD without internet (documented in the spa case, S50); settlement timing facts (S48); APIs exist if needed (S5). Do not claim integrations that are not documented | **H** |

Note: in many target accounts this role will not exist as a person. Ask who sets up and fixes the till today.

## 4. End users: cashiers, front-desk and floor staff, outlet managers

| Aspect | Description | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Responsibilities** | Take payments, issue receipts, close shifts, handle customer disputes, report takings | **I**. **V** that a front desk manager speaks for the spa (S50) |
| **Business priorities** | Quick, error-free customer payment; no blame for mismatches; simple screens | **V** (spa: staff time lost fixing payment problems, S50) |
| **Pain points** | Slow or failed payments; customers arguing that they paid; extra work at shift end | **V** (S50). **V** that Hubtel describes the "I paid, there is no record" dispute (S3) |
| **Buying motivations** | Less friction and fewer disputes. Possibly anxiety about being monitored | **H** |
| **Likely objections** | Another system to learn; fear that transparency will expose errors or informal practices; trust in new tools | **H** |
| **Influence** | Low on purchase, **high on adoption** and on whether the owner keeps the tool | **I** |
| **Messaging that would resonate** | Faster, simpler payments for customers; fewer disputes; customer-visible receipts (Hubtel's Control page says customers get receipts, S3) | **H** |

## 5. Internal influencers

| Influencer | Role in the decision | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Outlet managers | Recommend or resist based on daily experience; carry the rollout | **H** |
| Head cashier or senior staff | Informal veto on adoption | **H** |
| Co-owner or family member | Second opinion on risk and cost | **H** |
| External accountant or auditor | Trusted adviser on records and tax | **H** |
| Peers in other businesses | Referrals and reputation. The strongest likely source of trust in this market | **H** |

## 6. Potential blockers

| Blocker | How it blocks | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Incumbent provider or bank terminal contract | Lock-in, bundled terminals, switching cost | **H** |
| Owner's fraud and trust concerns | Reluctance to move settlement to a new platform | **V (snippet-level)** that fraud concerns are a cited barrier (S24) |
| Tax visibility concerns | Digital records make sales visible. E-levy cited as a constraint (S24, snippet-level) | **V (snippet-level)** |
| Staff resistance | Transparency reduces informal practices | **H**. Handle with care in messaging |
| Cost sensitivity | No public pricing (S52, S53) means no way to pre-empt the question | **V** that pricing is not public |
| Switching effort | Retraining, new till flow, downtime during busy periods | **H** |
| Hubtel's public controversy | ECG audit coverage (S42, S43) may surface in due diligence | **V** that coverage exists. **H** that owners care |
| Competing offers | Paystack, Flutterwave, ExpressPay and others hold the same licence tier (S16). Mobile money merchant tools and bank terminals are indirect alternatives (**H**) | **V** (S16) |

## How the committee changes by segment

| Factor | Hospitality (primary) | Retail (secondary) |
|---|---|---|
| Economic buyer | Owner or MD. In hotels, possibly a general manager with authority (**H**) | Owner or MD |
| Functional buyer | General manager or finance lead | Finance or operations lead |
| End users | Front desk, waiting staff, bar and kitchen cashiers | Shop attendants and shop managers |
| Distinct concern | Guest experience at payment; peak-time failures | Stock and supplier payment control, branch comparison |

## What a GTM engineer should do with this

1. Treat role titles as **search terms to test**, not facts. Verify against real account pages and LinkedIn before building lists.
2. Aim first contact at the **owner or managing director** for small groups and the **finance or operations lead** for larger ones. Test both.
3. Plan for a champion who is not the buyer: the finance lead or general manager.
4. Prepare for staff adoption objections as a second conversation after the purchase decision.
5. Keep messaging tied to **documented** capabilities only.
