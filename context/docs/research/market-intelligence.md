## Executive summary

Ghana's digital payments market is large and growing quickly, driven by mobile money. Bank of Ghana data reported in the press puts 2025 mobile money transaction value at about GHS 4.5 trillion, up about 50% from 2024 (S23, search summary; primary report not yet read). But the volume sits mostly in person-to-person and agent flows. Merchant acceptance is a different and smaller market: reported POS terminals were about 18,000 in 2025, and only about 37% of firms had adopted digital payment systems in 2024 survey data (S24, search summary). The gap between mobile money scale and merchant-grade tooling (reconciliation, multi-branch control, payouts) is the space Hubtel's business offer addresses (S1 to S3).

### 2. Merchant-side digital adoption (Fact as reported; low-to-medium confidence)

- POS terminals: 18,117 in 2025, up 16.2%; about 80 per 100,000 adults (S24, search summary).
- POS transactions: 29.21 million (2024) to 104.07 million (2025), up 77.3% (GhIPSS, S24; promotional source).
- Firm adoption: 37% of businesses had adopted digital payments in 2024 survey data, concentrated in Greater Accra and regional capitals (S24).
- Barriers cited: fraud concerns, uncertainty about returns, taxation (e-levy), security (S24).
- GhQR is positioned as a low-cost alternative to POS for micro merchants (S24).

### 3. Regulation

- Bank of Ghana licenses payment service providers in tiers. The approved list shows Hubtel, Paystack, Flutterwave, ExpressPay, Cellulant, Fincra and Payswitch under **PSP Enhanced** (S16). A Flutterwave Enhanced licence also appears in a news headline (search result, not opened).
- Practical implication: licensing is not a differentiator among the larger players. All named gateways can claim a regulated status.
- Not researched: PSP Enhanced scope, capital requirements, and any 2026 rule changes. **Gap.**

### 4. Adjacent markets (context only)

- Online food delivery: Glovo exited Ghana in May 2024 and Jumia Food in December 2023 (S25). Bolt Food and Chowdeck were reported active in 2024; no 2026 market share found. One projection puts 2024 revenue at about US$224.6M, from a market-research source not verified. Relevant to Hubtel Food (consumer side), not to the business payments offer.
- E-commerce: no reliable 2025 or 2026 Ghana e-commerce size was gathered. **Gap.**

### 5. Where B2B demand appears strongest (inference from the evidence)

Hubtel's own pain statements point to businesses with **multiple branches, multiple payment channels, staff handling money, and owner-visibility problems** (S2, S3). These are structural features, not an industry list. The evidence in hand suggests candidates, not conclusions:

| Candidate | Evidence for | Evidence against or missing |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-branch retail and pharmacies | Branch-reporting pain in S2, S3 | No Ghana retail sizing or Hubtel retail case |
| Restaurants and hospitality | Staff, cash and split-bill features; Food app heritage | No restaurant case study; competitive POS market |
| E-commerce | Developer APIs, invoice links | Crowded with Paystack and Flutterwave; Hubtel itself says it is not for simple collection |
| Large enterprise and utilities | Three headline case studies (insurance, power, betting) | Long cycles, anonymous proof, ECG sensitivity, small named-buyer universe |
| Financial services (insurance, microfinance, agent networks) | Insurance case study; verification APIs; bill payment APIs | Case bodies unread; regulatory complexity |
| SMEs generally | Largest count of businesses | 37% digital adoption; Hubtel says it is not for those who "just collect mobile money" |

All of the above are **Assumptions pending customer research** (see customer-intelligence.md).

## Confidence level

- Mobile money growth direction: **High**. Exact figures: **Medium** (relayed, two versions).
- Merchant adoption figures: **Low to medium** (secondary, some promotional).
- Segment attractiveness: **Low** (inference, no direct segment data).

## Knowledge gaps

- Read the Bank of Ghana Payment Systems Annual Report 2025 directly (S17).
- Merchant payments (not P2P) share of digital value.
- Number of registered businesses in Ghana by size and sector (Ghana Statistical Service, Registrar General).
- Card-acceptance, QR and POS pricing norms.
- 2026 regulatory changes.

## GTM implications

1. The market story is "mobile money exploded, merchant tooling lags". That is a reasonable thesis, but the headline GHS trillions must not be used as a Hubtel TAM.
2. Segments should be chosen by structural pain (branches, channels, staff handling money), which Hubtel itself names, not by industry label.
3. SME as a blanket segment is weak evidence: low digital adoption, and Hubtel disqualifies pure collection.

## What changed

- **No change to market sizing or competitor pricing.** The Bank of Ghana 2025 Payment Systems report (S17) was **not opened** in this pass, and no competitor's official pricing was retrieved. All Pass 1 market and competitor findings keep their original confidence levels.
- **Hubtel's own fee statement is now sourced.** Hubtel states its standard merchant fee is 1.95% per transaction, of which 1% goes to mobile money and card scheme providers and Hubtel keeps 0.95% (S40, S41, 2024). This upgrades the earlier C-grade listing of 1.95% (S29) to a Hubtel statement. It is a 2024 statement made in the ECG context, and Hubtel's fee page (S52) returned no content, so the current merchant rate card is **still unverified**.
- **Hubtel's settlement claim.** Hubtel says its merchant balance is credited hourly, every day, and that major Ghanaian gateways typically settle next business day (T+1), making Hubtel "roughly 96% faster" (S48). This is a **Hubtel comparison claim**. Competitor settlement terms were not checked against official sources. One Pass 1 blog source said Paystack and Flutterwave settle T+1 (C-grade), which is consistent but not verified.
- **Market share.** The CEO said about 12% of transaction volume, which is roughly 2% of value (S37, S39). A separate interview says "roughly 10 to 12% of the Ghanaian market" with no metric (S36). Both are Hubtel statements. No independent share data found.
- **Value vs volume.** The 12% volume vs 2% value pair suggests Hubtel's average transaction is small relative to the market. Inference only.
