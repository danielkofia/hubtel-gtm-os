# Buying Signal Matrix: Hubtel Business Payments and Back-Office Offer

**Owner:** Go-to-Market Engineering | **Audience:** GTM, Sales and leadership | **Version:** 2026-W46
Companion: [signal-scoring-framework.md](signal-scoring-framework.md). Builds on the [ICP](../../icp/ideal-customer-profile.md), [buying committee](../../icp/buying-committee.md) and [qualification framework](../../icp/icp-qualification-framework.md). S# = source ID in the [source register](source-register.md).

## Summary

This matrix lists 20 external events that may show an owner-led, multi-outlet Ghanaian business has a problem Hubtel can solve. It is for **primary ICP** accounts (hospitality and food service, 2 to 15 outlets, Greater Accra) and **secondary ICP** accounts (multi-branch retail). For each signal it says what the signal means, how to detect and verify it, how strong it is, what to say, what could make it misleading and what to do next.

Signals are not equal. They fall into four classes:

| Class | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Explicit intent** | The business is asking for a solution | A public request for a POS recommendation |
| **Strong** | Points to a problem or an imminent change Hubtel can address. Can justify research on its own | New outlet opened, public frustration with settlement |
| **Weak** | Gives context. Raises priority only when combined with other signals | New leader, funding news, renovation |
| **Interesting, not actionable** | Worth logging. Scores nothing | Awards, follower growth, general press |

Three rules drive the whole matrix:
1. **A signal is a reason to research, not a reason to contact.** Contact follows qualification under the [qualification framework](../../icp/icp-qualification-framework.md).
2. **Pain evidence matters most.** A growth signal tells us when to look. A pain signal tells us why they might buy.
3. **Only promise what Hubtel can do today.** Outreach angles use documented capabilities only: hourly settlement and sweeps (S48), approvals and payroll (S46, S47), POS for cash, card and mobile money (S51), USSD without internet (S50). Branch dashboards, per-staff tracking, automated reconciliation and audit logs need Product confirmation first.

## Evidence and limits

- **Signal strengths are our assumptions (H).** No signal has been tested against real Hubtel win or loss data. Sales and Commercial can tell us which of these preceded real deals. That is the fastest way to calibrate.
- **Data sources are candidates.** Coverage and accuracy for small Ghanaian businesses have not been tested. The scoring framework includes a test plan.
- **Case-study support is thin.** The only named small-business buyers are a hotel (slow settlement, S51) and a spa and salon (platform failures, no-internet use, S50). Both are Hubtel-written.
- **Data protection.** Collect business-level data only. Ghana data-protection obligations for contact data need Legal review before any list is built.

## Signal cards

Each card follows the twelve fields requested: name, description, why it matters, relevant ICP, problem indicated, where detected, data source, verification, intent strength, outreach angle, false positives, next action. "Candidate sources" are untested for coverage. Outreach angles are directions, not copy.

### Growth and expansion

**SG-01 New outlet opened or announced** (Strong, High)
1. **Name:** New outlet opened or announced.
2. **Description:** A business with one or more outlets opens another, or announces a date or location.
3. **Why it matters:** Hubtel's pain language depends on several outlets: figures that arrive on different days and do not match the bank (S2, S3). A new outlet is the moment the owner stops seeing everything personally and the till set-up is decided.
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** Loss of daily visibility of takings across sites. Need for a till and settlement set-up at the new site.
6. **Where detected:** Social media pages, Google Maps listings, local press, the business website, event and launch posts.
7. **Data source:** Facebook and Instagram business pages, Google Maps new listings, business websites, local news and directories (all coverage untested).
8. **Verification:** Confirm the outlet address on two independent sources (for example the business's own page plus a map listing or press). Check the opening date. Confirm it is the same business, not a similar name.
9. **Intent strength:** High (our assumption). The trigger is concrete and time-bound.
10. **Outreach angle:** Setting up payments at a new site before it opens. Money from each outlet in one place. Only claim documented features: POS for cash, card and mobile money (S51) and hourly settlement as "Hubtel states" (S48).
11. **False positives:** Pop-up or temporary stand. Outlet is a franchise run by a different owner. Announcement never happens. Business is already a Hubtel customer or already committed to a bank terminal contract.
12. **Next action:** Run the gates (G1 to G8), check the suppression list, record outlet count, search for pain evidence (SG-07 to SG-09), identify the owner or general manager. If gates pass, score and move to Research now or Monitor.

**SG-02 Entry into a new city or region** (Strong, Medium)
1. **Name:** New city or region entry.
2. **Description:** A Greater Accra business opens or announces its first site outside the region, or a regional business enters Accra.
3. **Why it matters:** Distance makes visibility harder, and a new location has to be set up from scratch.
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** Remote oversight of cash and settlement. Need for a payments set-up that works with variable connectivity.
6. **Where detected:** Press, social posts, business website, listings.
7. **Data source:** Same as SG-01.
8. **Verification:** Confirm the location and that Hubtel operates in that city (the homepage says 8+ cities, S1). Check with Commercial that coverage is real for that city.
9. **Intent strength:** Medium. Moves are slower and may be outside the first campaign geography.
10. **Outreach angle:** Running payments across cities. Payments that keep working when the internet does not (USSD, documented in S50) if connectivity is a concern.
11. **False positives:** A pop-up, event stall or catering job, not a permanent site. City outside Hubtel's coverage. Business is a franchise of a larger group.
12. **Next action:** Confirm city coverage, then treat as SG-01. If the city is outside Hubtel's coverage, log the signal and do not contact.

**SG-03 Multi-site group formation** (Strong, Medium)
1. **Name:** Franchise, second brand or acquisition that creates a multi-site group.
2. **Description:** A single-site business becomes a group through a second brand, franchise agreement or acquisition of another operator.
3. **Why it matters:** The owner suddenly runs two sets of tills, staff and payment habits that need to be combined or standardised.
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** Inconsistent payment set-ups across sites, consolidation effort, an approval trail across outlets.
6. **Where detected:** Press, business announcements, social posts, LinkedIn.
7. **Data source:** Local business news, LinkedIn company pages, owner interviews.
8. **Verification:** Confirm the transaction or agreement with the business's own statement. Check the combined outlet count.
9. **Intent strength:** Medium. Integration decisions come after the deal closes.
10. **Outreach angle:** One payment and approval set-up across the combined group. Control over who approves money out (documented: S46, S47).
11. **False positives:** Rumoured acquisition. Combined group exceeds 15 outlets or has central procurement (different buyer). The acquirer is a large corporate group.
12. **Next action:** Check the combined size against the ICP range. Research who now owns finance decisions. Revisit in 30 days to see whether the systems question is open.

### People and hiring

**SG-04 Hiring for finance, accounts or reconciliation** (Strong, Medium)
1. **Name:** Finance, accounts or reconciliation hiring.
2. **Description:** A public job post for an accountant, accounts officer, finance manager, bookkeeper or reconciliation role.
3. **Why it matters:** Hubtel describes matching mobile money, bank and cash across outlets as the problem (S2). A business hiring for it has probably outgrown manual work.
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** Manual reconciliation burden, payroll and supplier payment workload.
6. **Where detected:** Job boards, company social pages, LinkedIn.
7. **Data source:** Ghanaian job boards, LinkedIn jobs, Facebook and Instagram posts (coverage untested).
8. **Verification:** Read the job description for words like "reconcile", "daily sales", "branch reports", "payroll". Confirm the employer is the business itself and not a recruiter or an unrelated company with a similar name.
9. **Intent strength:** Medium. A hire could replace the need for a tool as well as create it.
10. **Outreach angle:** Reduce the manual work the new finance hire inherits. A clear record of who approved what (documented: S46, S47). Do not claim automated reconciliation.
11. **False positives:** Routine replacement of a leaver. Generic "accountant" role at a corporate group. Recruiter ad for another employer. The role is audit or tax only.
12. **Next action:** Keep only posts that mention multi-outlet or cash reconciliation work. Add pain evidence and check gates. Then score.

**SG-05 Hiring wave for outlet managers or cashiers** (Weak, Low)
1. **Name:** Outlet manager or cashier hiring wave.
2. **Description:** Several job posts for managers, cashiers or front-desk staff in a short period.
3. **Why it matters:** A hiring wave usually means a new site or a busier existing one.
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** More staff handling money, more risk of mismatches, and expansion that has not yet been announced.
6. **Where detected:** Job boards, social posts.
7. **Data source:** Same as SG-04.
8. **Verification:** Look for the reason (new site, new shift, seasonal peak). Count distinct roles and outlets.
9. **Intent strength:** Low. Many causes, only some relevant.
10. **Outreach angle:** None on its own. Use as a prompt to look for SG-01.
11. **False positives:** Seasonal hiring. Turnover. Agency posting for several clients.
12. **Next action:** Check for a new-outlet announcement within 30 days. If none, log and monitor.

**SG-06 New senior leader** (Weak, Low)
1. **Name:** New general manager, operations head, finance head or successor.
2. **Description:** A public appointment of a new senior leader.
3. **Why it matters:** New leaders review tools and suppliers, usually in their first 90 to 180 days.
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** A review of operations and finance processes. A possible sponsor for change.
6. **Where detected:** LinkedIn updates, company announcements, press.
7. **Data source:** LinkedIn, company pages, local news.
8. **Verification:** Confirm the person's role and start date. Check that the appointment is at the target business, not a sister company.
9. **Intent strength:** Low. Many appointments change nothing.
10. **Outreach angle:** If contact is made later, acknowledge the new role and offer a short walkthrough of what Hubtel states about approvals and settlement. Do not use as an opener without a pain signal.
11. **False positives:** Promotions that change titles but not decisions. Appointments at a group holding company with central procurement.
12. **Next action:** Record the buyer name and start date for later use. Look for pain evidence. Do not contact on this signal alone.

### Observed pain and payments

**SG-07 Public complaints about payment failures or slow service at the till** (Strong, Medium)
1. **Name:** Public till complaints.
2. **Description:** Customers post reviews or comments about failed payments, slow checkout or "I paid but they cannot see it".
3. **Why it matters:** Hubtel describes the "I paid, there is no record" dispute (S3). The spa case shows staff time lost fixing platform failures (S50).
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** Unreliable terminals, poor connectivity at the till, no clear payment verification.
6. **Where detected:** Google reviews, Facebook and Instagram comments, TripAdvisor-style review sites, X posts.
7. **Data source:** Public review and social pages (coverage untested).
8. **Verification:** Look for at least two complaints about payment specifically, in the last 90 days, from different people. Check that the business replied or not.
9. **Intent strength:** Medium. Complaints show a problem, not a decision to change.
10. **Outreach angle:** Payments at the counter that work when connectivity is poor (USSD, documented: S50). A record of each payment. Use as private insight. Do not quote customer reviews in outreach.
11. **False positives:** One-off complaint. Complaint is about food or service, not payment. Fake or competitor-driven reviews. Problem already fixed by the business.
12. **Next action:** Save two or more review links with dates. Check for a provider mention (SG-08). Score pain evidence and proceed to full qualification.

**SG-08 Public frustration with a payment provider, terminal or settlement** (Strong, High)
1. **Name:** Provider or settlement frustration.
2. **Description:** The owner or staff publicly complains about a provider, terminal or slow settlement, or customers see a "payment system down" notice.
3. **Why it matters:** This is the nearest observable match to the hotel case, where funds took one to three weeks with a previous provider (S51).
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** Slow access to money, unreliable terminals, willingness to switch.
6. **Where detected:** Owner posts, interviews, notices at the till, community groups, comments.
7. **Data source:** Social pages, press interviews, photos of notices, public groups.
8. **Verification:** Confirm the speaker is the owner or a manager of the business. Note the provider named, if any. Record the date.
9. **Intent strength:** High. It names the problem and often the provider.
10. **Outreach angle:** Faster access to funds, shown as what Hubtel states (hourly crediting and sweeps, S48). Acknowledge the problem without criticising the named provider.
11. **False positives:** Venting after a one-day outage. A complaint about a bank charge that is not about the payment tool. Personal accounts with no link to the business.
12. **Next action:** Qualify now. Note the incumbent provider and any contract hints. Prepare a human review of the account within the week.

**SG-09 Several different payment instructions shown to customers** (Strong, Medium)
1. **Name:** Multiple payment instructions.
2. **Description:** Menus, posters or posts that list several mobile money numbers, bank accounts or "pay to this number" instructions.
3. **Why it matters:** Hubtel names "checking MoMo and bank apps" as the verification problem (S2). Many numbers mean many places to check.
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** Manual payment verification, fragmented money across wallets and accounts, staff handling numbers.
6. **Where detected:** Social images, menus, receipts, website payment pages.
7. **Data source:** Public photos and posts.
8. **Verification:** Check that the instructions are current and for the business (not a personal number). Count distinct instructions.
9. **Intent strength:** Medium. Shows a symptom, not a decision.
10. **Outreach angle:** One place to see money from all channels. Do not claim reconciliation automation.
11. **False positives:** Different numbers for different services that are managed well. One owner with two wallets. Old posters.
12. **Next action:** Add as pain evidence. Combine with SG-01 or SG-04 for priority.

**SG-10 Payment channel change** (Strong, Medium)
1. **Name:** Payment channel change.
2. **Description:** A business starts accepting card, POS or QR, announces "cashless", or removes a payment method.
3. **Why it matters:** It shows active payment decisions. It also moves accounts past gate G5 (accepts or wants digital payments).
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** Active evaluation of providers. A new set-up that may have been chosen without the full picture.
6. **Where detected:** Social announcements, notices, photos of counters, press.
7. **Data source:** Public posts and photos.
8. **Verification:** Find out which provider or terminal is in the photo. Confirm the change is permanent.
9. **Intent strength:** Medium. The decision may be done already.
10. **Outreach angle:** What happens when a channel fails. Settlement timing (S48).
11. **False positives:** Promotional "pay with card" offers. Competitor decal on a terminal means the decision is already made.
12. **Next action:** If a competitor decal is visible, set a 6-month review. If not, treat as an open evaluation and research.

### Technology and partnerships

**SG-11 Technology investment** (Strong, Medium)
1. **Name:** POS, ERP, booking or accounting tools adopted.
2. **Description:** The business announces or shows new POS, ERP, property management, booking or accounting software.
3. **Why it matters:** Active modernisation. It is also a point where integration needs appear.
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** Operations being modernised. Possible need to connect payments to the new system (APIs exist, S5).
6. **Where detected:** Company posts, vendor case studies, LinkedIn, job posts that name tools.
7. **Data source:** Vendor announcements, social posts, job descriptions.
8. **Verification:** Name the tool and the date. Check whether payments are included in the tool.
9. **Intent strength:** Medium. The tool may include payments, which could displace us.
10. **Outreach angle:** Payments that connect to what they already use. Only mention integrations that Product confirms.
11. **False positives:** The tool is a closed system with its own payments. The announcement is a pilot.
12. **Next action:** Ask Product which tools Hubtel connects with. Treat as a timing signal. Combine with pain evidence.

**SG-12 Online ordering, e-commerce or online booking expansion** (Weak, Low)
1. **Name:** Online channel expansion.
2. **Description:** The business adds online ordering, delivery, an online shop or online booking.
3. **Why it matters:** It adds payment flows. It is also outside the ICP if the business becomes online-only.
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary, only where the counter business remains.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** New online payments to manage alongside counter payments.
6. **Where detected:** Website, social posts, app listings.
7. **Data source:** Websites, social pages.
8. **Verification:** Check whether payments are handled by a platform (delivery app, booking site) or by the business.
9. **Intent strength:** Low. Platforms usually own the payment.
10. **Outreach angle:** Use only if the business takes payments itself. Otherwise none.
11. **False positives:** Delivery handled entirely by a third-party platform.
12. **Next action:** Log. Look for SG-01 or SG-09 in the same period.

**SG-13 New partnership** (Weak, Low)
1. **Name:** New partnership.
2. **Description:** A partnership with a delivery platform, booking platform, franchisor, supplier or payments provider.
3. **Why it matters:** It changes payment flows and can tie the business to another provider.
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** Either a new flow to manage or a lock-in that reduces our chance.
6. **Where detected:** Press, social posts, partner pages.
7. **Data source:** Press releases, social posts.
8. **Verification:** Read the announcement for payment terms. Note any exclusivity.
9. **Intent strength:** Low. May be negative if it names a competing payment provider.
10. **Outreach angle:** None on its own.
11. **False positives:** Marketing partnerships with no payment link.
12. **Next action:** If the partner is a payments provider, record the incumbent and set a review in six months.

**SG-14 Funding or investment** (Weak, Low)
1. **Name:** Funding or investment.
2. **Description:** Public news of capital raised or an investor taking a stake.
3. **Why it matters:** Capital often funds expansion and systems.
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** Planned expansion and a stronger need for reporting that an investor will expect.
6. **Where detected:** Press, LinkedIn.
7. **Data source:** Business and finance news.
8. **Verification:** Confirm the investor and amount from at least one credible source.
9. **Intent strength:** Low. No link between funding and a payments decision.
10. **Outreach angle:** Investor-ready numbers across outlets, once capability is confirmed.
11. **False positives:** Grant or loan for a purpose unrelated to operations. Rumours.
12. **Next action:** Look for SG-01 or SG-03 in the following 90 days.

**SG-15 Renovation, relaunch or rebrand** (Weak, Low)
1. **Name:** Renovation, relaunch or rebrand.
2. **Description:** A site closes for renovation, reopens, or relaunches under a new brand.
3. **Why it matters:** A reopening is a natural moment to change till systems.
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary, hospitality in particular.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** Upgrade of guest-facing systems.
6. **Where detected:** Social posts, press.
7. **Data source:** Public posts.
8. **Verification:** Confirm reopening date and scope.
9. **Intent strength:** Low.
10. **Outreach angle:** Payments ready for reopening day. Use only with another signal.
11. **False positives:** Cosmetic changes.
12. **Next action:** Log. Check again when the site reopens.

### Explicit intent

**SG-16 Public request for a POS or payment recommendation** (Explicit, High)
1. **Name:** Public request for a solution.
2. **Description:** The owner or manager asks in a public post, group or comment which POS or payment provider to use, or what to do about a payment problem.
3. **Why it matters:** The buyer is asking. This is the strongest signal in the matrix.
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** An active, stated need.
6. **Where detected:** Public business groups, social platforms, community forums, comments on Hubtel's own pages.
7. **Data source:** Public posts and comments. Hubtel's own social and inbound channels are the best-controlled source.
8. **Verification:** Confirm the poster represents a business (not a private person) and that the need matches Hubtel's documented capabilities. Respect the platform's terms and privacy.
9. **Intent strength:** High.
10. **Outreach angle:** Answer the question asked, using documented capabilities (for example USSD without internet, S50). Offer a short call.
11. **False positives:** A student or private individual. A reseller or competitor asking. A closed group where replying would be intrusive.
12. **Next action:** Fast lane. Check suppression and gates, then human review within two working days. Reply publicly only if the platform and group allow it.

**SG-17 Vendor selection, RFP or procurement notice** (Explicit, High)
1. **Name:** Vendor selection or RFP.
2. **Description:** A published call for POS, payments or cashless solutions, or a business saying it is choosing a provider.
3. **Why it matters:** A decision is under way.
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary. Most RFPs come from larger organisations, so check ICP size.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** An active procurement.
6. **Where detected:** Procurement portals, press notices, business announcements.
7. **Data source:** Public tenders and notices.
8. **Verification:** Confirm the issuer, deadline and requirements. Check whether the issuer exceeds the ICP range.
9. **Intent strength:** High, but often a large-account motion.
10. **Outreach angle:** A response to the stated requirements.
11. **False positives:** Large groups outside the ICP. Closed tenders already awarded.
12. **Next action:** Route within the ICP to the fast lane. Route larger issuers to Commercial, because large accounts are outside this campaign.

**SG-18 Referral or warm introduction** (Explicit, High)
1. **Name:** Referral.
2. **Description:** A Hubtel customer, partner or staff member introduces a business that has a need.
3. **Why it matters:** Peer referrals are the likely strongest source of trust in this market (buying committee, H).
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** A stated need passed by someone who knows the business.
6. **Where detected:** Sales, Customer Success, partners.
7. **Data source:** Hubtel internal records.
8. **Verification:** Ask the referrer what the need is and whether the business agreed to be contacted.
9. **Intent strength:** High.
10. **Outreach angle:** Reference the introducer only with their permission.
11. **False positives:** A referral that is a courtesy with no need.
12. **Next action:** Fast lane. Log the referrer and the stated need.

### Hubtel-side and seasonal

**SG-19 First-party engagement with Hubtel** (Strong, Medium)
1. **Name:** First-party engagement.
2. **Description:** A business visits Hubtel's business pages, requests information, signs up for BackOffice, or follows or messages Hubtel's business channels.
3. **Why it matters:** Interest is shown directly to us. As an in-house team we can see this data; an outside team could not.
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary and secondary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** Early research on solutions.
6. **Where detected:** Hubtel website analytics, BackOffice sign-ups, inbound messages, support.
7. **Data source:** Hubtel internal systems. Access, consent and lawful use need confirmation with Legal and Data.
8. **Verification:** Match the visitor to a business. Check that the visitor is not an existing customer or a competitor.
9. **Intent strength:** Medium. Depends on pages visited and repeat visits.
10. **Outreach angle:** Follow up on the exact pages or form used.
11. **False positives:** Students, competitors, existing customers, people looking for the consumer app.
12. **Next action:** Match to the ICP and suppression lists. Score. If the business signed up with a clear need, treat as explicit intent.

**SG-20 Seasonal or event peak preparation** (Weak, Low)
1. **Name:** Seasonal or event peak preparation.
2. **Description:** A hospitality business announces a festive season, wedding season, conference, festival or similar peak.
3. **Why it matters:** Peaks stress tills and settlement.
4. **Relevant ICP:** Primary.
5. **Problem it may indicate:** Peak-time payment failures.
6. **Where detected:** Social posts, event listings.
7. **Data source:** Public posts.
8. **Verification:** Confirm the event dates and size.
9. **Intent strength:** Low. Seasonal.
10. **Outreach angle:** Payments that hold up during peaks, only with another signal.
11. **False positives:** Routine promotions.
12. **Next action:** Log. Use as a timing modifier for accounts already scored.

## Interesting, but not commercially actionable

Log these. They score nothing and do not trigger outreach.

| Event | Why it is not actionable |
|---|---|
| Awards, rankings, "best of" lists | Reputation, not a payments or operations change |
| Social follower or review growth | Popularity, not a problem |
| General press features on the owner | No operational detail |
| New menu, new service or promotion | Does not change payments |
| Hiring chefs, stylists, sales staff | Not related to money handling |
| Sponsorships and charity events | Not related |
| Closure or sale of one outlet in a large group | Group is outside ICP range or shrinking |
| General sector news (new tax or regulation) | Not account-specific |

## Disqualifying and suppressing signals

When any of these appear, stop and record the reason.

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| Account is on Hubtel's customer list | Exclude from acquisition targeting |
| Account has fewer than two outlets and no expansion plan | Exclude (gate G4) |
| Competitor decal or recent public announcement of a payments partner | Suppress for six months, then recheck |
| Business is a Hubtel Food or marketplace partner only | Different motion, exclude |
| Business appears closed or inactive | Exclude (gate G1) |
| Part of a large group with central procurement | Exclude from the initial campaign |
| Regulatory or legal trouble reported | Hold. Do not contact until reviewed |

## Confidence

- Signal list and structure: medium.
- Intent strength ratings: low (assumptions, untested against Hubtel win data).
- Data source coverage: low (untested).

## Knowledge gaps

- Which of these signals preceded Hubtel's actual small-business wins. Ask Sales and Customer Success.
- Coverage of Ghanaian business listings, job boards and social pages for target businesses.
- Ghana data-protection rules for collecting and using contact data.
- Hubtel's first-party data: what exists, who owns it and whether it can be used for acquisition.
- Which tools Hubtel integrates with (affects SG-11).
