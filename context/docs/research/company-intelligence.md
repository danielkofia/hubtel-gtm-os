# Company Intelligence: Hubtel

> **Pass 2 notice (2026-11-13):** a validation pass is appended at the end of this file. It corrects or supersedes parts of the text below. The original text is kept as an audit trail. See the correction log in source-register.md.

Independent educational research, not affiliated with Hubtel. Access date: 2026-11-13. Source IDs (S#) refer to [source-register.md](source-register.md). Labels: **Fact** (sourced), **Claim** (company or CEO statement, unverified), **Assumption**, **Unknown**.

## Executive summary

Hubtel is a Ghana-based, privately held payments and commerce company that began in 2005 as SMSGH, a bulk SMS provider, and moved into payments from about 2015. It holds an Enhanced Payment Service Provider licence from the Bank of Ghana (on the regulator's approved list, S16). It runs two kinds of business under one brand: a business-facing payments and back-office offering, and consumer apps for bills, food and stores. Its own homepage now leads with the business side and says it is for "scale-ready businesses" and not for those who "just need to collect mobile money" (S1). The company has publicly reported fast growth (FT 2026 ranking, S18) but also a public dispute over its ECG revenue-collection contract (S20, S21).

## Findings

### 1. History (Fact, with date conflicts)

- Founded 2005 as SMSGH by Alex Bram, Ernest Apenteng and Leslie Gyimah (S26, search summaries). Early product was bulk SMS.
- Micro-billing via airtime reported in 2011; Hubtel app 2012 (search summary, single-source).
- Pivot to payments reported around 2015 (search summary; Hubtel's own "pivot" retrospective exists but was not read).
- Rebrand to Hubtel dated 2017 (S26, citing a GhanaWeb report of 13 May 2017). One Hubtel source ties the rebrand to 2016 (search summary). **Conflict: 2016 vs 2017.**
- Licence: initial approval August 2020, full Enhanced PSP licence April 2022 (S11, S22). **Another search summary said "2019". Treat 2019 as unsupported.** Wikipedia (S26) says 2022.
- Closed offices in Kenya, Nigeria and Cameroon to focus on Ghana (CEO account via search summary, S15 area). Claim, single source.
- Co-founder Leslie Gyimah has died; Hubtel published a tribute (search summary). Not researched further.
- Headquarters Accra (S26). Staff "over 700" (search summary, unverified).

### 2. Business model: B2B and B2C together

Hubtel is **both**, with different buyers (S1):
- **Business** (Clarity, Control, Growth pages S2 to S4, Developer APIs S5, BackOffice App): the buyer is an owner or finance and operations lead running multiple branches or channels.
- **Consumer** (Food, Stores, Pay Bills, Send SMS, Pay Small Small): the buyer is an individual user. Also "Serve with Us": retail partners who sell on Hubtel, and riders (S1).

The consumer apps also act as a distribution and supply side: retailers who sell on Hubtel are B2B-adjacent. Bills collection (e.g. ECG) is B2B2C: a utility is the customer, the public pays.

### 3. Products (Fact: from Hubtel's pages)

- Payment acceptance: MTN MoMo, Telecel Cash, AT Money, bank, cash, POS, card (Visa and Mastercard), GhQR, invoice links, split bill (S1, S3).
- Payments out: payroll, supplier payments, refunds and transfers with owner approval and audit log (S3).
- Reconciliation and visibility: every transaction tagged by channel, branch, staff and time; one dashboard across branches (S2, S3).
- Developer APIs: receive mobile money and card, transfer to wallets and banks (GhIPSS network), bill payments (ECG, airtime and data, water, TV), customer verification (Ghana ID, Voter ID, phone, bank account) (S5).
- Bulk SMS (heritage product) and SMS tracking (S1, S5).
- **Pricing: no pricing on any Hubtel business page read.** Unknown.

### 5. Customer stories (headline only)

Three case studies exist on Hubtel's news site (S6 to S8): an insurance company (84% more policies per agent), Ghana's largest power distributor (collection below 22% of potential to processing GHS 51 billion), and a pan-African betting company (GHS 162B). **None names the customer, and the article bodies were not retrieved.** A fourth anonymous claim on the Growth page says "5x growth" (S4). All are Hubtel marketing claims. The "GHS 51 billion" case appears to correspond to ECG, but Hubtel does not name it in the headline; this is an **Assumption**.

### 6. Risk and reputational context (Fact from news, with caveats)

- Auditor-General 2024 report (as reported, S21): Hubtel earned about GH¢325M in service fees over three years collecting ECG revenue; ECG could not produce supporting documents; a GH¢101M claim recommended for disallowance; GH¢75M paid for a digital platform without a signed contract.
- Hubtel's position (S20): its fee is 0.95% of a 1.95% charge, and a US$25M figure was a board-approved ceiling with actual spend of US$12M. The audit coverage cites a 1.5% commission. **The two rates were not reconciled.**
- An invoice of about GH¢259.8M was reportedly reduced to GH¢20M (S26 and search summaries; blog-grade sources).
- Parliamentary outcome: none found. Not researched in depth.
- A fact-check reported Hubtel is owned by Alexander Adjei Bram and Ernest Kwesi Apenteng (search summary).

This matters for GTM: any message that leans on the utility or government collections story invites this context. Treat it as a sensitivity, not a reason to avoid the company.

### 7. Positioning and messaging (Fact, from S1 to S4)

- Frame: three steps, Clarity then Control then Growth.
- Voice: owner pain in first person ("I check MTN. I check the bank app. I ask the customer for a screenshot. I'm still not sure.").
- Explicit qualification: "for scale-ready businesses", "not for businesses that just need to collect mobile money".
- Strapline: "20+ years of connecting businesses to communities."
- Claimed outcomes are directional with no numbers on the product pages themselves.

## GTM implications

1. Hubtel has a deliberately narrowed business-side message: owners with multiple branches or channels who want visibility and control, not simple collection. That qualification should shape who counts as a fit.
2. The proof is thin and unnamed. A GTM engineer should not borrow the case-study numbers in copy until verified.
3. The ECG controversy is a known sensitivity for any government, utility or large-collections pitch.
4. No pricing is public, so offers should avoid price claims.

# Validation Pass 2 (2026-11-13): company claims re-tested

Pass 1 text above is preserved. Where this section differs, this section supersedes it. Source IDs S32 onward are in [source-register.md](source-register.md). Corrections are numbered C1 to C10 there.

## Executive summary

Reading the actual article text (not headlines) changed the picture in three ways. (1) Hubtel's most detailed proof is three **bespoke enterprise builds** (a power distributor, a gaming operator, an insurer), not a repeatable self-serve product. (2) Several scale figures can be tested against Hubtel's own case-study numbers, and two of the Pass 1 figures (1 million daily transactions, 15,000 merchants) have no retrievable source and are dropped from working facts. (3) The conflicts on revenue, ECG fees and funding remain **unresolved**, but each is now traced to who said what.

## Findings: the conflicting figures

### 1. Businesses served: 7,979+ vs about 15,000

| Figure | Where it appears | Status |
|---|---|---|
| "7,979+" businesses | Hubtel homepage (S1) | Hubtel claim. Definition of "business" not given. Re-fetch needed to see if the number changes over time |
| "about 15,000 merchants" | Known only via a search summary of Villpress (S27). Page is suspended. Not in the Citi, HBTV or Hubtel versions of the CNN interview (S37 to S39) | **No retrievable source. Dropped from working facts** |
| "over 800,000 such businesses" in the economy | CEO, CNN interview, as reported by Citi and Hubtel (S37, S38) | Fact that the CEO said it. "Such businesses" is not defined. Implies Hubtel's reach is small relative to its stated market (see inference below) |

Inference (labelled): 7,979 against 800,000 is about 1%. This only holds if both figures count the same kind of business, which is not established.

Status: **7,979+ = unverified Hubtel claim. 15,000 = unsupported.**

### 2. Daily transactions: over 1 million vs 3.5 million

- 3.5 million: stated in a Hubtel-published interview write-up dated 2026-01-29 (S36). Verified as **Hubtel's statement**, not independently verified.
- Over 1 million: only from the suspended Villpress page. **Unsupported.**
- Consistency check using Hubtel's own case studies (arithmetic by me, not a Hubtel statement): the gaming company alone is reported at 725.0M transactions in 2025, about 2.0M a day (S35); the power distributor at 65.9M in 2025, about 0.18M a day (S34). Together about 2.2M a day, which is already above 1 million. So "1 million" cannot be Hubtel's current total if those case-study figures are right.
- Implication (inference): a large share of Hubtel's transaction count may come from one high-frequency gaming client (about 57% of 3.5M a day if the periods were comparable, which they may not be). Transaction count therefore says little about merchant or retail activity.

Status: **3.5M = Hubtel claim, internally plausible. 1M = dropped.**

### 3. Revenue

| Figure | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 63.68M for 2024; 17.91M for 2021; 52.63% CAGR; FT 2026 rank 48 | MyJoyOnline, 14 May 2026 (S18) | Reported by a news outlet citing the FT list. **Currency not stated in the text I read.** FT list itself not opened |
| "over $110 million in annual revenue" | CEO to CNN, as written by HBTV Ghana, 4 Oct 2025 (S39) | Quote confirmed in one outlet. The Citi and Hubtel versions of the same interview (S37, S38) do **not** contain it |

Possible explanations (none verified): different year (2024 vs 2025), different currency, or different measure (for example gross fees vs net revenue). Do not use either figure as "Hubtel's revenue" without stating its source. **Unresolved.**

New related fact: the CEO said 12% of transaction **volume** equals roughly **2% of total value** (S39). That means Hubtel's average transaction is small. A sanity check by me: Hubtel's gaming and power case-study flows for 2025 total about GHS 67B (52.53B plus 14.48B), roughly 1.5% of the Bank of Ghana mobile money value of GHS 4.54 trillion (S23). That is the same order of magnitude as the 2% claim, but it is **not a verification** (different definitions, partial customers).

### 4. ECG fee: 0.95% vs 1.5%

What each party actually says:
- **Hubtel (S40, S41):** its standard merchant fee is 1.95% per transaction; 1% goes to mobile money and card scheme providers and Hubtel keeps 0.95%. "Hubtel's recurring fee for managing ECG's new commercial systems is 0.95%." It denies taking 3%. Contract term: 5 years. Costs: a US$25M ceiling set by ECG's Board; about US$12M spent so far. ECG owns the licences and intellectual property.
- **Auditor-General, via press (S43, snippet only):** Hubtel collected over GH¢10.3 billion (GH¢10,344,056,338.64 for the 2023 financial year in one outlet) and deducted a **1.5%** commission before depositing the rest. One outlet gives the commission as GH¢148.9 million, which is about 1.44% of that collection (my arithmetic). Total fees about GH¢325M over three years. A GH¢101M claim recommended for disallowance.

Why they may not conflict (hypothesis, unverified): the Auditor-General's figure concerns the 2023 financial year, before the services agreement was executed in March 2024 (S42); Hubtel's 0.95% is stated for the agreement and the standard merchant fee. They could also differ in scope (1.5% gross vs 0.95% net of scheme fees). I have not read the audit report or the agreement.

New conflict found: Hubtel says the GH¢75M was paid in **2023** and that no payment was made in 2022 (S42). Press coverage of the audit reports the payment as **2022** and says there was no signed contract (S43).

Status: **Unresolved. Do not quote a single ECG fee rate.** Hubtel's own 1.95% / 0.95% split is a verified **Hubtel statement** for the standard merchant fee.

### 5. Funding

- CB Insights lists one unattributed venture round dated 2021-08-02 with Chanzo Capital as investor and no amount (S44, snippet only).
- The "self-funded, no VC" claim comes only from the suspended Villpress page (S27). No Hubtel statement on funding was found.
- Hubtel says no government official or political party affiliate has ever owned its shares (S41). A fact-check summary (Pass 1) named two owners (Bram, Apenteng).

Status: **Unknown.** Treat "self-funded" as unverified. Treat "has outside investors" as a single database entry.

## Findings: what the enterprise case studies actually show (now read)

| Case | Customer | What Hubtel built or provided | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insurance (S33, 2026-06-26) | Unnamed insurer | Agent Portal, Customer Portal, Staff Portal (branch dashboard), website refresh, AI chatbot. Every Agent Portal transaction is attributed to the agent | The "84%" compares 50 portal agents with 462 others (see C3). Collections through the portal grew from GHS 3,865 (Oct 2024) to GHS 1.67M (July 2025). No quotes from the customer |
| Power distributor (S34, 2026-06-24) | Unnamed. Matches ECG (S40) | Unified payment framework, PowerApp, Oracle 10G to 19C upgrade, 24/7 monitoring, consumption monitoring, OperationZero anomaly detection, management dashboard. Engagement began Nov 2022 | Hubtel's flow figures: GHS 51.03B across 159.2M transactions, July 2022 to May 2026. No quotes |
| Gaming (S35, 2026-06-25) | Unnamed | Receive Money, Send Money, USSD, SMS, Balance Query and Transfer APIs, Merchant Dashboard. Hubtel was the company's first processor in Ghana | GHS 162B total flow (deposits plus disbursements) Jan 2021 to May 2026. No quotes |

Findings:
- These are **custom engineering and integration projects with large clients**, with Hubtel's own staff running monitoring and software. They are not evidence of a product that a retailer can buy and use unaided.
- The power distributor's own commercial scale (GHS 14.48B in 2025) and gaming (GHS 52.53B in 2025) dwarf the other documented customers by orders of magnitude. If Hubtel's total revenue is roughly 63M to 110M in some currency, these accounts likely matter a great deal, but **no revenue split by customer or segment exists in the evidence.**
- Concentration risk is a reasonable inference, not a finding.
